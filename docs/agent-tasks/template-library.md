Owner, 2026-11-19: "the template characters must be the most current, interesting plots young people like
(take the plots from PolyBuzz); research which plots and which gender is in demand, set the templates' gender
by the data; the code and server must count for real who used which template, openly, so it trends."
Builds on `docs/agent-tasks/creator-ecosystem.md` (phases 3-4, reshaped by the owner: templates the user can
make their own, with their own picture).

Rule kept (`AGENTS.md`, `docs/cast-2.md`): we take the **type** of a plot, its numbers and its shape, never
another creator's text, name or picture. Every template below is written by us.

## 1. What the data says

**Who uses these apps.** Character.AI's audience is about even by gender (third-party panels: 50.4-51.4 %
male, 48.6-49.6 % female) and young (about 52 % aged 18-24)
([electroiq](https://electroiq.com/stats/character-ai-statistics/),
[demandsage](https://www.demandsage.com/character-ai-statistics/)). No public gender data for PolyBuzz.

**Who they talk to.** The characters are mostly men. In a random sample of 100 public Character.AI
characters: 50 single men, 17 single women, 12 ungendered, the rest groups (Lee & Joseph 2025,
[arXiv 2505.13354](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.13354v1)). The most-chatted are male leads: a billionaire
CEO 215M chats, a CEO boss 158M, a mafia husband 151M, a bad-boy best friend 135M (same paper, Table 2). Our
own count on Character.AI (`docs/cast-2.md` §2) agrees: male-lead plots 129-304M, the best female-lead plots
40-81M. Romance-community studies put women at the large majority of AI-romance users
([arXiv 2605.08650](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.08650v1)); general surveys show men and women using AI
partners at similar rates (Wheatley Institute 2025, via
[okcfox](https://okcfox.com/news/nation-world/emotional-availability-without-the-drama-women-turn-to-ai-for-companionship-artificial-intelligence-dating-relationships-boyfriend-girlfriend-husband-wife-spouse-computers-web-mobile-apps-depression-anxiety-emotional-intimacy-love)).

**Reading it.** Women write the romance chats and pick a male lead; men are as many in the app but spread
over fewer plots (girlfriend, crush, ex, roommate). So the library leans male-lead, not 50/50.

**Which plots.** Biggest topics among 3M Character.AI characters (characters per topic): school friendship
and secret crush 73K, fights with a boyfriend 30K, arranged marriage 29K, royal arranged marriage 22K,
mafia arranged marriage 12K, CEO and assistant 8K, sports rivals 5K (arXiv 2505.13354, Table 4). Our counts
(`docs/cast-2.md` §2): best friend with a secret crush 304M chats, arranged marriage with a cold husband
196M, brother's best friend 179M, the bad boy only you calm 176M, possessive husband / boyfriend 164-170M,
boss / CEO 129M, fake dating an enemy 129M, enemy roommate 111M, bodyguard 83M, the crush who turned you
down 81M, an ex who wants you back 79M. TikTok saves: friends to lovers, situationship and boyfriend POV
lead. PolyBuzz's own tabs (A51, 0z292): For you, Redemption, Slow burn, Classmates.

What we leave out: school age (the adult version instead: "back home after years"), a pregnant user, real
people, franchises, and cruelty (jealousy is a feeling a character owns and admits, never control, threats
or harm; explicit scenes fade to black as in every chat).

## 3. Real counts and trends (server math, no fake numbers)

Every number on a card is real. A new template starts at 0: below 10 the card shows "New" instead of a
count, so a quiet card never looks dead and never lies.

- **What counts**: one account starting a chat with a template ("Say hi") or making it their own ("Make
  it yours"), **once per account per template**, whichever came first. Opening the card does not count.
- **Where**: templates live in `community_characters` with `kind = 'template'` and no owner (one table,
  one card shape, one counter path); `community_adds (user_id, community_id, created_at, how)` keeps one row
  per account (`unique (user_id, community_id)`, already there), `how` = `hi` | `own`.
- **Counters** on the row: `chats` (all time), `chats_7d` (recomputed every hour by a scheduled SQL job:
  count of adds in the last 7 days), `owns` (made-it-yours count).
- **Shelf order**: "For you" = trending score `chats_7d + 0.1 × chats` (fresh activity wins, all-time
  breaks ties); inside a chip the same score; "New" = newest first. User-shared characters (the community
  library) join the same shelves with the same math once they pass review.
- **Card shows**: `chats` ("12.4K chats", formatted per locale) and, on the sheet, `owns` ("1.2K made it
  their own"). A small "Trending" mark on the top 3 of a shelf by `chats_7d`.
- **Abuse guard**: counts only signed-in accounts older than 24 h; the server ignores a burst over 30 adds
  a minute from one account; one account's own share never counts its own adds.
- **Cost**: no model call; one indexed insert per first chat, one hourly SQL update.

## 4. Build order

1. Write the 100 templates (Claude writes, Gemini translates to 14 locales; a brief in `docs/agent-tasks/`).
2. Migration: `kind`, `tropes text[]`, `chats_7d`, `owns`, `how`, the hourly job; seed the 100 rows.
   Owner applies it (`node scripts/apply-migration.mjs …`) and deploys the server once.
3. Server: `GET /v1/community?trope=&sort=trending|new&offset=`; `POST /:id/add` takes `how`.
4. App: Find's chips and two-column cards (a coloured initial in place of a picture until pictures come), the card
   sheet with Say hi and Make it yours (opens Create filled in; the user's own picture and edits, private).
5. Drive it on the A51 (Russian too), then HANDOFF + flows.
